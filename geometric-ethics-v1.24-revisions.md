### Note on the Geometric Series (v1.24)

Since this book was first drafted, the structures discovered here — the heuristic field, the gauge group, the stratified manifold, the search-theoretic interpretation of moral deliberation — have turned out to be instances of a more general architecture. *Geometric Reasoning: From Search to Manifolds* (Bond, 2026c) develops the general theory: reasoning, human and artificial, is informed search on a structured manifold, with quality determined by the geometry of the heuristic field and failures characterized as geometric pathologies. This book is the first domain instantiation of that general theory.

The relationship is bidirectional, and the direction matters. The structures in this book were not derived from the general theory — they were discovered here, in the moral domain, and the general theory was built to accommodate them. The $D_4$ dihedral group was found by testing every transformation on thousands of moral scenarios. The conservation of harm was inferred from cross-lingual invariance patterns. The A* interpretation of moral reasoning emerged from the tensor hierarchy and then proved to generalize. The parent text, *Geometric Reasoning*, owes its architecture to the patterns first identified in these pages.

This book remains entirely self-contained. Nothing in it depends on the general theory, and readers who have no interest in the broader program will lose nothing by ignoring it. But for readers who find the moral-domain structures suggestive — who wonder whether heuristic fields, gauge invariance, and geodesic deviation have analogues in economics, law, cognition, or clinical reasoning — the companion volume develops those connections explicitly.

A companion empirical program, the Measuring AGI benchmarks, has since confirmed several predictions of the framework across five cognitive domains (social cognition, learning, metacognition, attention, and executive functions) with significance levels from 4.6$\sigma$ to 13.3$\sigma$. The 8.9$\sigma$ framing effect — the finding that surface reformulation shifts moral judgment by 14-23% of the evaluation scale — is the empirical signature of heuristic corruption (Chapter 5 of *Geometric Reasoning*) measured in the moral domain. The 13.3$\sigma$ sycophancy gradient — the finding that AI systems' deference to social pressure varies continuously from 0% to 56% — is the empirical signature of objective function hijacking applied to a moral context. These results are reported in full in *Geometric Reasoning* Chapter 13, but they began as tests of predictions made by the framework in this book.

This explains why evolution pre-compiled these heuristics into our cognitive architecture: admissible heuristics guarantee that search reaches the optimum, and exact moral computation is intractable (Theorem 11.2). ## Appendix G: Relationship to the Geometric Series

This book is the first domain instantiation of a general framework for structured reasoning. The framework is developed across a series of volumes, each applying the same mathematical architecture to a different domain. This appendix maps the relationship.

### The Series

**Tier 1: The Toolkit.** *Geometric Methods in Computational Modeling* (Bond, 2026a) develops the mathematical apparatus — manifolds, metrics, curvature, persistent homology, group-theoretic augmentation, SPD manifolds, topological data analysis, hyperbolic geometry — without committing it to any subject.

**Tier 2: The General Theory.** *Geometric Reasoning: From Search to Manifolds* (Bond, 2026c) claims that reasoning is informed search on a structured manifold, develops a failure taxonomy (heuristic corruption, objective hijacking, local minima, gauge breaking), and grounds the theory in empirical data from the Measuring AGI benchmarks (5 tracks, 5 models, 20 tasks, headline significances from 4.6$\sigma$ to 13.3$\sigma$).

**Tier 3: Domain Instantiations.** Each domain text takes the general framework and instantiates it on a domain-specific manifold. This book — *Geometric Ethics* — is the first.

### What This Book Contributes to the General Theory

The relationship is not one-directional. Several structures discovered in this book — in the moral domain, from moral data — were subsequently recognized as instances of general patterns:

1. **The A* interpretation** (Ch. 11). The discovery that moral reasoning is A* search on the moral manifold, with obligation vectors as gradient vectors of heuristic functions, directly motivated the general theory's central claim that all reasoning has this structure.

2. **The $D_4$ group** (Ch. 8). The discovery that Hohfeldian jural relations exhibit the symmetry of the dihedral group of a square was empirical — found by testing every transformation against thousands of scenarios. The general theory's prediction that every reasoning domain has a gauge group was motivated by this specific discovery.

3. **The conservation of harm** (Ch. 12). The derivation of a conserved quantity from the BIP via Noether's theorem provided the template for domain-specific conservation laws. The general theory predicts that each domain has a conserved quantity; the moral domain's harm conservation is the first to be derived and tested.

4. **The stratification** (Ch. 8). The Whitney stratification of moral space, with its semantic gates and phase transitions, suggested that reasoning spaces in general may be stratified rather than smooth. The general theory's treatment of gauge invariance benefits from this insight but does not require it — stratification is a domain-specific feature.

5. **The No Escape Theorem** (Ch. 18). The proof that geometric constraints on AI moral evaluation cannot be circumvented by representational manipulation is the strongest result in the series. It is domain-specific (it requires the moral manifold's particular features) and does not have a known general analogue. Whether domain-specific No Escape theorems exist for economics, law, or other domains is an open question.

### What This Book Inherits

In return, the general theory provides this book with a broader context:

- The **failure taxonomy** (corruption, hijacking, local minima, gauge breaking) was developed in *Geometric Reasoning* by abstracting from the moral domain's specific failures. This book's framing effects are an instance of heuristic corruption; its contraction analysis is related to the Scalar Irrecoverability Theorem; its alignment-as-invariance principle is an instance of BIP-as-necessary-condition.

- The **empirical methodology** (eight types of geometric probes) was derived by systematizing the specific tests developed here and in the Measuring AGI program. The Dear Abby analysis is retrospectively recognized as a combination of invariance and sensitivity probes.

- The **engineering toolkit** (group-theoretic augmentation, adversarial training, LoRA fine-tuning) provides implementation strategies for the DEME architecture that this book describes but does not fully develop.

### The Other Domain Books

The general theory predicts that the architecture discovered here — manifold, metric, tensors, gauge group, conservation law, stratification, search-theoretic deliberation — instantiates in other domains:

- **Geometric Economics** (in preparation): The Bond Geodesic Equilibrium generalizes Nash equilibrium to manifold-valued decision spaces. GDP is a lossy contraction of the economic tensor.
- **Geometric Law** (in preparation): The $D_4$ Hohfeldian group has its natural home in law. Equal protection is gauge invariance. Sentencing disparities are gauge violation tensors.
- **Geometric Cognition** (in preparation): System 1/System 2 as heuristic-dominated vs. geodesic search regimes. The Measuring AGI benchmarks provide the empirical foundation.
- **Geometric Communication** (in preparation): Translation as parallel transport. Cross-lingual invariance as the communication BIP.
- **Geometric Medicine** (in preparation): The QALY as lossy contraction. Triage as forced tensor contraction.

Each domain book inherits the general framework and adds domain-specific structure that the general theory cannot anticipate. The accumulation of domain instantiations — each tested against its own data — is the empirical program of the series.
